Common questions

Asked on most first calls.

How much capital do I actually need to start?

It depends entirely on the channel. Arbitrage can start small because you buy stock in short cycles and recycle the cash quickly. Private label needs the most, since you are paying for inventory, packaging, samples and a launch before a single unit sells. Wholesale sits in between. On the call we work backwards from what you have rather than pushing you towards the model with the biggest fee.

Do I need a US LLC or UK Ltd company to sell?

Not always to open an account, but it changes what is possible. A registered company affects which payment providers and banks will work with you, how you are taxed, and whether you can file a trademark and enrol in Brand Registry. Setting it up before you scale is far cheaper than restructuring afterwards.

How long does trademark registration take?

Filing takes days. Examination and publication take months, and the exact timeline depends on the office and whether anything is queried. We handle the clearance search, the filing and any office actions, and tell you at the start what is realistic for your name and classes.

Who owns the accounts and the brand?

You do, without exception. The company, the marketplace accounts, the trademark, the supplier relationships and the data are all in your name. We work inside them as your team. If you stop working with us, nothing leaves with us.

Can you take over a store that already exists?

Yes, and it is a large part of what we do. We start with an audit of listings, pricing, ad spend, inventory and supply chain, then tell you which single constraint is actually holding growth back before we touch anything.

Do you guarantee results?

No, and be careful with anyone who does. Marketplace outcomes depend on product, capital, competition and timing. What we commit to is the work, the reporting rhythm and an honest read on whether a plan is realistic — including telling you when it isn't.

The vocabulary

Six numbers we watch
on every account.

If any of these are new to you, that's fine — they're explained on the call rather than used to sound impressive.

Metric

Landed cost

What a unit truly costs by the time it reaches the warehouse — factory price, freight, duty and prep, not just the quote.

Metric

Contribution margin

What's left after referral fees, fulfilment, storage and returns. The only profit figure worth planning around.

Metric

ACOS & TACOS

Ad spend as a share of ad sales, and of total sales. The second one tells you whether ads are building anything.

Metric

Buy Box share

The percentage of the day your offer is the default one. On a shared listing, this is the whole game.

Metric

Sell-through rate

How fast stock moves relative to what's held. Slow sell-through quietly turns your cash into shelf space.

Metric

Days of cover

How long current inventory lasts at the current run rate — the number that decides when to reorder.
